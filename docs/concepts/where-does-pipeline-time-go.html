TL;DR — what 100 minutes of wall time really are

Across 798 hours of wall time (847 tasks), time splits like this: ~38% waiting (queue, lease, restart orphaning, human reviews — no compute at all), ~41% redoing (retries), ~21% genuine first work. Put differently: four of every five minutes are either idle or redo. The largest compute block by far is the LLM coding step (66.8% of compute, 98% of all tokens) — tests are the second-largest by time but token-free. But "redo" is not "waste": most retries are the intended orchestrator quality loop, each carrying a tailored prompt (§4). So the highest-leverage optimization is neither "tests" nor "waiting", but: fewer, cheaper re-runs of the parts that did not change.

§3The retry tax — and what a "retry" is

Definition — an attempt is a full pipeline re-run, not a steering

An attempt is one full pipeline execution recorded in pipeline-execution.json; a retry is any attempt after the first (archived in previousAttempts). A new attempt opens only when the runner re-picks-up the card after a prior run already crossed the core/post boundary (EnsureAgentRunStart → HasReachedAgentRunBoundary). An in-run steering / continue — a message injected into the running CLI — stays inside the same core-agent-run step and does not increment the counter. The cause breakdown of the retries themselves is §4.

Retries are not one line item — they are a multiplier on everything. Each reissue runs the full post-step chain again:

If every task ran exactly once, 19,871 min of compute (66.7%) would vanish. That is by far the largest lever — bigger than tests, waiting, or token optimization. But before treating it as pure waste, read §4: most of it is the quality loop doing its job.

The dominant re-run cause is quality steering — with a tailored prompt

When the orchestrator judges a result not good enough, it does not simply re-run the same prompt. It emits a Steer/SoftIntervention message and writes a fresh adapted prompt to orchestrator-follow-up-history/<timestamp>-reissue.md — the corpus holds 978 of these (avg 2.24 per reissued task, up to 16). Combined with 78% findings-added outcomes, this says the bulk of re-runs are the intended quality loop, not dumb repeats. The clearly-technical cause is a distinct, smaller bucket: 560 failed-post-processing events, almost all test-gate timeouts.

Worth its own evaluation — the adapted prompts are a corpus

Those 978 reissue prompts are a rich, un-mined dataset: what does the orchestrator actually ask for on a quality reissue? Are the instructions specific and actionable, or generic? Does prompt quality predict whether the next attempt passes? This directly touches whether the quality loop converges efficiently or spins — a task with up to 16 reissues is either a genuinely hard card or a loop that never sharpened its ask. Recommended follow-up: a research task that samples and grades the reissue prompts, correlating prompt specificity with attempts-to-green. Potentially the highest-value insight in this whole analysis.

Consequence for optimizations: saving time ≠ saving money. Cutting test-gate or wait time buys throughput but almost no budget. Cutting tokens means touching the coding step — i.e. fewer/shorter coding re-runs (see §3/§4), not the periphery.

§6Anomaly: post-abort-review

2,258 min over just 57 runs = ~40 min per run, at 0 recorded tokens. For an LLM evaluation step (ADR-0054, "intelligent abort assessment") that duration at zero tokens is implausible — the duration likely includes waiting on a downstream rerun, or a measurement error in step demarcation. Worth its own probe: either real lost time hides here, or this step's durationMs attribution is wrong — either way you want to know before trusting the numbers for steering inside the tool.

§7Does this belong in the tool?

Your open question. Arguments sorted:

ForAgainst / caution
The data is already structured (pipeline-execution.json) — it is a read/aggregate view, no new capture effort. It measures our own machinery, not the user's product. Risk of building a dashboard only the three of us look at.
"Retry tax" and "waiting" are control variables: they show where tasks stall, which cards are expensive, whether a runner is blocked. The most valuable finding (66% retry) is a process insight, not a live widget — you read it once, not daily.
It docks onto existing surfaces: pipeline-step-usage, the token ledger, the workspace token timeline. Absolute values drift with every run; a live board needs upkeep so it does not lie.

Recommendation on the tool question

Yes, but narrow and diagnostic, not a vanity dashboard. The one in-app value worth it: make what costs time visible per task — including retries and waiting, so expensive/stalled cards jump out. A project-wide aggregate is a nice-to-have and can live as a periodically generated report (like this page) rather than a live board. Concrete forms in §8.

Idea C — Project report "Where the time goes" report, not live

Exactly the waterfall + step bars of this page, but per project and generated periodically (e.g. a wiki page a post-step refreshes). No live poll, no upkeep burden — a snapshot that keeps the process insights (retry rate, wait share) visible.

Idea D — "Where is it stuck" signal diagnostic

Instead of numbers: a traffic-light hint when a step stands out — e.g. wait share > 50%, attempts > threshold, or a 0-token step with high duration (the post-abort-review anomaly from §6). Leads from symptom straight to cause.

Idea E — Reissue-prompt inspector ties to §4

Surface the adapted reissue prompts (*-reissue.md) on the task timeline, each pinned to the attempt it triggered. Lets an operator see why the orchestrator sent the task back — and is the UI side of the reissue-prompt evaluation recommended in §4.
