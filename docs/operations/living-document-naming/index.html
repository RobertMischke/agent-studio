01 · Naming problem

The noun must describe the same artifact before and after the operator decision. A name that fits only the center of the lifecycle is incomplete.

Why Living Doc stops short

Correct Category, Weak Product Noun

It truthfully covers the lifecycle and behavior, but it gives up distinctiveness. “Open the Living Doc” does not tell the operator whether to open the Dossier surface, the Wiki, a Markdown page, or external documentation. The collision is structural, not cosmetic.

Why Living Spec remains the runner-up

Best Rename, Not Best Overall Name

If the public noun must advertise task-driven freshness, Living Spec is the clearest compact option. Its cost is semantic: early exploration is not yet a specification, and a documented postmortem or operating record may never be one.

04 · Recommendation

Keep Dossier and Explain the Living Behavior

The second rename is not worth its attention cost. Dossier has a small discoverability gap, not a truth problem.

Explicit answer: no second rename

A dossier that grows is not absurd. The word denotes a collected body of material about a subject; it does not imply a frozen snapshot. In this product, the dossier gains analysis, decision receipts, linked delivery state, and final documentation over time. That behavior stretches the name, but does not break it.

Naming layer

Dossier / Dossiers

Short in navigation, distinctive beside Tasks, Epics, and Wiki, natural in English and German speech, and compatible with the stable AGT-W4 key model.

Behavioral explanation

One Sentence Does the Missing Work

Recommended public copy: “Dossiers bring analysis, decisions, delivery tracking, and durable documentation together, and stay current as linked work ships.”

The phrase living dossier may be used descriptively in long-form documentation, but it should not become a second official label or a longer navigation term. Product nouns need stability; behavior can be taught in copy.

05 · Evidence

Adjacent Practices Validate Pieces, Not the Whole

Established artifacts each optimize for a phase or knowledge shape. None of them combines all four jobs without borrowing or stretching its conventional meaning.

  • Shape Up pitchBasecamp defines a pitch around problem, appetite, solution, rabbit holes, and no-gos so a team can make an informed bet. That is an excellent model for the dossier’s shaping phase, but the pitch has done its naming job once the bet is made.
  • Amazon PR/FAQThe Working Backwards practice begins with a future press release and customer FAQ to clarify a desired outcome. It is a framing format for a proposed product, not a durable implementation and operational record.
  • RFC processesInternet-Drafts evolve, but the RFC Editor states that published RFC contents do not change; updates and obsoletions are separate metadata or documents. The familiar label therefore points in the opposite direction from task-driven live updates after publication.
  • Architecture Decision RecordsMichael Nygard’s original form intentionally stores one architecturally significant decision in a short modular record. That narrowness is its strength and the reason an ADR extension is the wrong umbrella for non-architecture exploration and delivery tracking.
  • Living DocumentationCyrille Martraire’s formulation spans goals, domain knowledge, architecture, design, process, and deployment through continuous investment in knowledge. It validates the product behavior, but “Living Documentation” is a discipline and category rather than a concise, collision-free UI noun.

The transferable lesson is structural: borrow pitch-like problem framing, RFC-like public review, ADR-like decision receipts, and living-documentation freshness. Do not inherit any one culture’s name when its lifecycle boundary is narrower than the product’s.
