§1AGT-2662 · Rebase, merge, and bounce steering

confidence: high

Dossier: docs/operations/rebase-merge-and-steering/

Recommendation

Keep the stage-specific Git hybrid and make its boundary explicit: a fresh agent recovery round rebases the delivery onto current origin/<integration-branch> and records a new fenced review subject; canonical integration tries SHA-preserving no-FF merge first, with mapped rebase only as a guarded fallback that fails closed on ambiguous attribution; promotion advances main only to the exact gated candidate SHA. Move the first recoverable conflict-skipped bounce from live-session labor into one deterministic, restart-safe backend round per operator review epoch (scenario 2). Invoke a strong guardian only on repetition, contradictory evidence, or genuinely semantic conflicts, ending at Human Review when the bounded budget is exhausted — no blind third loop. The Batch Gate (AGT-2648) stays a separate decision.

Evidence

  • Session ownership is the proven failure mode: on 13 August the steering session stopped and 17 eligible cards accumulated without requeue; 16 more piled up on 15 August; the live improvement log's 20 August entry states "session-cron mortality now bit three times"; on 25 August a tripped pickup circuit breaker with an empty halt reason left 0 claims from 00:20 to 09:00 with 32 escalated cards — stalls can be invisible, not just unowned.
  • The merge-first invariant is a learned correction, not a preference: the first 10 August recovery cut rebased before merging and made acceptance attribution fragile; the 11 August AGT-2632 amendment reversed the order — preserve SHAs first, map only as exact fallback, roll back before publication, project ambiguity as AgentRoundRequired. Squash or unmapped rebase at canonical integration is forced out by the commits[] ancestry acceptance contract.
  • Scenario 2 extends deployed seams, not a new scheduler: POST /api/tasks/{jobId}/integration/rebase already validates and requeues; IntegrationAgentRoundService already runs one bounded automatic round; AGT-2544's envelope policy already does one deterministic retry then typed escalation; AGT-2654's guardian already writes restart-safe receipts.
  • Honest weak spot: no measured bounce-volume or cost baseline exists — slice S0 (shadow mode) exists precisely to measure eligible count, latency, repeats, and false eligibility before any mutation. The Batch Gate member-assembly shape is explicitly undecided.

Cost of delay

First-bounce ownership currently equals session availability: each session death strands eligible reviewed cards (17 on 08-13, 16 on 08-15, three session-cron mortality incidents by 08-20, and the invisible breaker pause of 08-25). The 30-day lane-transition measurement sizes the surrounding manual carousel: 354 operator-requeue moves = 95 days of cumulative rework (median 1.9 h each), most of it the orchestrator's own rebase-requeue rail reacting to develop movement — though only the integration-recovery share (42 moves) maps one-to-one to the rail this decision automates. Undecided means every future orchestrator-session death repeats the pileup shape, since implementation cards are cut only after the operator picks a scenario.

On approval

Migration slices S0–S4, backend-owned via existing services: S0 defines the IntegrationBounceObligation contract and shadows current manual bounces without mutation; S1 gives the deterministic rail ownership of the first eligible reviewed conflict-skipped round (matrix-tested; no model call); S2 adds floor-aware thinking selection, action receipts, a queue-age alert, and a per-project kill switch — acceptance includes the 17-card session-loss shape draining after a backend restart with no open Studio session; S3 feeds repeated or complex bounces into the W15 guardian evidence pack, one strong call per fingerprint; S4 aligns with the Batch Gate only if AGT-2648 is approved. Defaults: one automatic round per operator review epoch, claim-or-visibly-defer within five minutes; automation never resolves content, edits Git, waives a gate, or requeues a hold card. The card is concept-only — implementation cards follow the pick.

Open questions

  • Batch Gate member assembly (mapped rebase replay vs. prebuilt no-FF member merges) is undecided inside AGT-2648 — shadow-compare both before the pilot chooses; a fixed rebase replay makes the per-member replacement map a launch-blocking evidence contract.
  • How far automation may act: append a precise steer, apply a floor-safe route, supersede, and requeue via existing services — or only durable detection and obligation visibility, with mutation declined.
  • Guardian trigger boundary: strong model on repeats and semantic conflicts (at least Sol/medium, Sol/xhigh for fencing/security/data-loss risk, one call per fingerprint) — or straight to Human Review; the model may author explanation and proposed steer, never the lane or Git mutation.

§2AGT-2652 · Token and retry tax, 11–12 August

confidence: high

Dossier: docs/operations/token-retry-tax-analysis/

Recommendation

Approve a CAR requeue-continuation slice first: persist the captured Codex session UUID per attempt chain, resume it only when task identity, worktree, branch, and provider still match, and cap a mechanical resumed rebase round at 1,211,213 tokens (10% of the cold-round envelope). On the observed workload the central counterfactual saves 19.8 weekly quota points (prudent range 17.6–19.8). In parallel, finish the incident controls already in flight — AGT-2646, AGT-2649, AGT-2651 were still in auto-review at cutoff; only AGT-2650 is live as agt-2650b — and claim no recurrence protection until they are live and a controlled restart/deploy smoke proves it.

Evidence

  • Of the 27 observed quota points (68%→95%, 23:52–09:24 CEST): 3 points (11.1%) useful first-run coding, 22 points (81.5%) conflict/rebase re-rounds, 2 points (7.4%) aborted/duplicate/probe runs; reviews consumed 0 GPT points. 88.9% of the rise was retry or aborted-run tax.
  • 8,540 Codex token_count events between the anchors: 1,091,939,651 processed tokens, priced by TokenEconomy 0.3.2 at an API-list equivalent of $743.64 — of which $564.81 was conflict re-rounds across 61 sessions.
  • A cold rebase-only round (median 12,112,130.5 tokens, N=10) cost 3.9% more tokens and ran 26.1% longer than a first coding run (median 11,653,720 tokens, N=9) — the opposite of a mechanical continuation's desired shape; every re-round opened a new rollout id because resumeSessionId was null in nightly runner specs, despite codex-cli 0.144.1 supporting exec resume.
  • Review incidents (1,905 LeaseExpired messages, wedged-worker kills, a 160-start crash loop) cost host time but 0 GPT points; the conflict-bounce tax (22 of 24 retry points) is untouched by the in-flight incident controls and needs the continuation or a platform-rebase control.

Cost of delay

If this exact wave recurs once per weekly quota window, the non-overlapping central saving forgone is 21.8 quota points per week (19.8 continuation + 1.0 deploy smoke + 1.0 helper race), with 18.6 points/week as the stated planning floor. In observed terms: one such night burned 27 points of the weekly GPT quota, of which 24 points (about $625 API-equivalent of $743.64 total) were tax rather than work.

On approval

P0: pilot the CAR attempt-chain session-continuation slice on rebase-only rounds (persist session UUID, identity-gated resume, compact requeue delta, one resumed continuation with typed fallback, per-generation ledger fields); in parallel approve the design of a platform-owned deterministic rebase preflight (up to 22.0 points — overlaps continuation, never summed); finish review and ship AGT-2651 (deploy smoke). P1: ship AGT-2649 (MainPID wait) and AGT-2646 (attempt re-adoption), keep AGT-2650 live, approve the token-ledger durability slice (card tokenSummary was null on all inspected nightly cards). P2: decide AGT-2648 separately on CPU/latency grounds.

Open questions

  • The 10% resumed-round envelope (≤1,211,213 tokens, ≤300 s) is an engineering SLO, not a demonstrated property of the untested resume path — the 17.6–19.8-point saving is the estimate the pilot must validate; model-substitution scenarios (13.2 or 21.12 points) overlap it and must not be added on top.
  • AGT-2649's 1-point attribution is inferred: the link between the config-helper PID race and the first aborted AGT-2635 run is "probable, not proven".
  • The literal 22:00 CEST window held at least 32 points, not 27 — the dossier answers only the 68%-to-95% question; recurrence is not closed until AGT-2646/2649/2651 are live and a controlled restart/deploy smoke passes.

§3AGT-2648 · Batch Gate

confidence: medium

Dossier: docs/operations/batch-gate-concept/

Recommendation

Adopt Option A, Batch Gate, as the primary throughput control: keep per-card model review and immutable delivery validation, but move the full build/test suite into one single-flight run over a closed, fenced, exact-SHA combined candidate, publishing only a verified green tip (red candidates reduced by bounded halving; conflicted or superseded members ejected without blocking the rest). The operator note of 18 August sharpens the sequencing: the full suite never runs per round — model review and cheap deterministic checks run first on every delivery, and the suite runs only as the final gate once a card appears done, once per batch; the note claims roughly half of today's suite runs (rounds that die at review or on conflicts) would be eliminated outright. Approval requested is for the operating contract plus a reversible docs-only pilot — explicitly not yet approval to edit the current pipeline. Option C (separate gate host) composes with A later; Option B (risk-scaled skipping) must never be the only guard for code-bearing changes.

Evidence

  • 11 August night (the trigger): 12-core host at load 22.7 (~1.89/core) versus the admission threshold of 1.5/core, with 4 concurrent full review suites plus coding, drain of ~4 reviews per 2 hours, and a 41-card waiting queue — concurrent full suites, not test cost, are the wave-scale bottleneck.
  • 25 July (847 tasks, 2,396 attempts): coding carried ~98% of all tokens and test CPU was ~0.05% of token spend — the dossier explicitly does not claim tests became expensive; batching targets host concurrency, not money.
  • Bounded red path: one reproducible offender is isolated in at most ceil(log2 n) diagnostic suite runs; the bound does not hold for multiple offenders or cross-member interaction failures, which fall back to per-task gates or an operator-owned cohort.
  • The operator note's "roughly half of today's suite runs" savings figure is stated without a cited measurement.

Cost of delay

Every delivery wave repeats the 11 August pattern: 4 concurrent full suites throttle coding and drain reviews at ~4 per 2 hours — a 41-card queue needs roughly 20 hours of wall time, and the fleet's post-processing wait already sits at a 1.2-day median. The pilot targets quantify what delay leaves on the table: ≥50% fewer overload minutes, ≥50% faster drain at comparable arrivals, suite runs per delivered member down to ≤0.40 (versus ~1 today, before counting the per-round runs the operator note calls wasted). Caveat the dossier itself makes: one observed night window, not a controlled benchmark.

On approval

Four predeclared slices under the shared ref-mutation lease: (1) shadow manifest — one week or ten eligible docs-only deliveries, batches formed and persisted without changing routing; (2) docs-only authority pilot — 20 deliveries in batches of 2–4 through the real full suite, proving construction, exact-SHA publication, per-member evidence, restart recovery, rollback; (3) low-coupling code pilot — one allowlisted feature area, max batch size 4, only after docs-only go-criteria; (4) general decision after at least three comparable wave windows. Prerequisite: a native append-only per-member Batch Gate record (the 11 August integration-records endpoint is bookkeeping, not gate-pass authority). Pilot parameters: close at 4 members (hard max 8), 15-minute age trigger, pressure-close at 2 when queue ≥12 or the load gate throttles. Rollback is per project and never rewrites develop; any evidence-correctness violation stops the pilot.

Open questions

  • Core capacity evidence is a single night's observation — the pilot must retain comparable windows and host telemetry before default routing changes; the "roughly half" savings figure carries no measurement.
  • Is the red-path budget acceptable: ceil(log2 n) diagnostic runs per isolation cycle with cohort fallback, given no logarithmic guarantee for multi-offender or interaction-only failures?
  • Sign off the pilot parameters as parameters (size 4 / max 8, 15-min age, pressure-close at 2, docs-only first) so success criteria cannot move after results; the suite-as-final-gate trigger ("card appears done, no further round expected") needs an operational definition.

§4TE-43 · TokenEconomy evidence base

confidence: medium

Dossier: C:/Projects/coding-agent-token-economy/docs/operations/evidence-base/ (entrypoint index.md)

Recommendation

TokenEconomy should recommend the least expensive evidence-qualified set of model-and-thinking routes that clears the task's correctness floor, rather than a single global winner. Concretely: add Planning and DecisionMaking as explicit strong-ideal task classes; split quota-independent recommendation (returns a set) from quota-aware selection (picks one route only with a current, trustworthy quota snapshot); treat MechanicalChore, DocEdit, tight local repair, and bounded transforms as smaller-model territory with explicit provisional labels; keep cross-file implementation, open-ended research, real PR review, frontend, planning, and decisions at a strong floor until task-local evidence shows non-inferiority. Add outcome metrics over retained attempts (billed tokens and dated list-price cost per accepted delivery, retry uplift, evidence-gated expected cost to acceptance), costed via the shipped v0.3.1/v0.3.2 pricing catalog at the attempt timestamp.

Evidence

  • SWE-Lancer (E02, rated A-, vendor bias high): 724 real manager/proposal-selection tasks with 99% validation agreement and no tested model at majority accuracy on the full set — the strongest direct evidence for strong-ideal DecisionMaking; higher reasoning effort raised pass@1 from 9.3% to 16.5% on the Diamond implementation subset.
  • TokenEconomy's own curated hard-coding suite (O02): Sol/medium 9/9, Terra/medium 2/9, Claude Sonnet 5/high 4/9 — supports a conservative strong floor for demanding Feature work (three synthetic C# fixtures only; catalog cost missing for two routes).
  • Aider polyglot (E04, rated B): o4-mini/high and Claude Opus 4 (32K thinking) tied at 72.0% two-attempt score at $19.64 vs $65.75 recorded run cost — direct evidence a smaller model can be economically equivalent on bounded coding; supports set-valued results and retry-adjusted metrics.
  • Review is not coding (E06 SWE-PRBench): eight frontier models detected only 15–31% of human-flagged issues diff-only, the top four statistically indistinguishable, every model degrading with more context; corroborated by SWRBench (E05) with up to 43.67% F1 gain from multi-review aggregation.

Cost of delay

No per-week figure; quota-aware selection savings are explicitly unmeasured (no counterfactual replay exists yet). Directional costs documented: continued flagship spend where a smaller route can tie (the observed tie cost $65.75 vs $19.64, roughly 3.3x, on a bounded distribution); the gap-closing benchmark wave stays uncommissioned, so no named smaller model can ever be declared equivalent for review, planning, decisions, or HTML work; and current single-winner recommendation copy keeps overstating what the evidence supports.

On approval

Five decisions, approvable independently: (1) strong-ideal semantics for Planning and DecisionMaking; (2) recommendation sets as the quota-independent API result plus a separate pure quota-aware Select; (3) the conditional smaller-model territory with provisional labels; (4) the efficiency-metric definitions with attempt-level inputs required before implementation; (5) commissioning the five-wave gap-closing benchmark program. Executable policy does not change from this dossier alone: TE-42 or a later policy card must version any change, following the stated compatibility path (add the two task classes, introduce RecommendSet beside SuggestModel, add Select with a RecommendationOnly state, version policy/JSON/knowledge/tests together).

Open questions

  • Per-class non-inferiority margins: no global delta is set deliberately (5 percentage points may suit reversible chores but not security/data-loss work) — margins must be declared per class before benchmark results exist.
  • Fate of the existing SuggestModel API: compatibility projection only if it can avoid inventing a winner, otherwise deprecate.
  • Sequencing and resourcing of the five-wave program, given that Planning/DecisionMaking strong-ideal (O08: no local benchmark) and the MechanicalChore/DocEdit smaller-ideal claim currently rest on operator risk judgment, not local data.

§5TE-42 · Model benchmark per task class

confidence: low

Dossier: C:/Projects/coding-agent-token-economy/docs/task-class-routing-studies.md

Recommendation

Route per task class from a versioned, set-valued evidence catalog instead of one global winner: the pilots recommend Sol/medium for HTML/UI implementation (Terra/medium only as a downgrade candidate for reversible, single-frontend-subsystem cards with deterministic visual regression and policy score ≤50) and Terra/medium for bounded source-code review with a compact contract, while a security boundary keeps a Sol/xhigh hard floor and ambiguous evidence that can authorize security/destructive/lane-affecting actions requires at least Sol/medium. Recommend() returns a ranked quota-independent set; Select() picks within it only from a fresh quota snapshot, and downgrades never cross correctness floors. Both class priors are explicitly provisional: with 2 scenarios and 1 attempt per route the pilots are stated as "not validated" until qualification thresholds are met.

Evidence

  • HTML/UI pilot (2 scenarios, deterministic checks + blinded Sol/xhigh jury): Sol/medium passed 2/2, mean quality 0.9175, 0 critical defects, $0.212954 per outcome; Terra/medium passed 2/2 at 0.86125 for $0.086544/outcome; Sol/xhigh passed 1/2 with 1 critical defect at $0.519849/passed outcome — "More thinking did not improve this pilot."
  • Source-code review pilot (2 C# snapshots, 10 seeded contract defects): Terra/medium passed 2/2, found 10/10 seeds, precision 0.8125, $0.030434/outcome; Sol/xhigh had equal recall at about 5.4x cost/outcome; Sol/medium passed only 1/2 with 9/10 seeds.
  • Weak-evidence disclosures the dossier itself makes: qualification requires ≥5 scenarios, 20 attempts per route, 70% outcome coverage, ≤10% semantic reissue; the Sol/medium HTML/UI cases consumed 31,074 additional jury tokens of unknown dollar cost, so $0.212954 is subject-only; the Quality Studio observational line shows 0 eligible runs for both routes.
  • Smaller tiers gpt-5.5/medium and gpt-5.4-mini/high are registered as "scheduled", not measured; one excluded review run (empty reviews under nested bubblewrap) is retained as an infrastructure failure, not negative model evidence.

Cost of delay

Per-outcome deltas, not a weekly rate: keeping bounded source review on Sol/xhigh costs about 5.4x per outcome ($0.164714 vs $0.030434), and HTML/UI on Sol/xhigh cost $0.519849 per passed outcome (passing only 1/2) versus $0.212954 subject-only on Sol/medium. Weekly cost scales with review/UI card volume, which the dossier does not state; delay also leaves gpt-5.5/medium and gpt-5.4-mini/high perpetually "scheduled" and the class priors stuck at unvalidated pilot status.

On approval

The next identical-scenario wave runs the already-registered gpt-5.5/medium and gpt-5.4-mini/high candidates; then six documented slices in order: security assessment (Sol/xhigh stays the hard floor), redundancy detection, graphical-quality judgment, consistency checking, expansion of existing coding classes, and Planning/Decision-Making corpora with blinded expert ground truth. Each class qualifies only at ≥5 scenarios, 20 attempts/route, 70% outcome coverage, ≤10% semantic reissue. Agent Studio consults the TaskClassRecommendationCatalog at card creation; ModelRouter, floors, and operator pins remain authoritative. The companion dossier TE-43 asks the operator to commission the gap-closing wave.

Open questions

  • Adopt the provisional pilot priors now or wait — is 2 scenarios × 1 attempt per route enough to start routing bounded review to Terra/medium?
  • True HTML/UI cost per outcome is unknown: 31,074 jury tokens were retained only as a total, so the published $0.212954 excludes verification cost.
  • Sol/medium missed a weighted review defect while cheaper Terra/medium found 10/10 — inversion signal or two-scenario noise? The smaller tiers are entirely unmeasured.

§6AGT-2661 · Organization-wide telemetry layer

confidence: medium

Dossier: docs/operations/telemetry-layer/ (AGT-W38)

Recommendation

Adopt a hybrid, local-first telemetry architecture: every organization app appends events to a durable app-local JSONL buffer, a collector validates/redacts/fingerprints/deduplicates and sends a best-effort loopback push to Agent Studio, which emits derived observations on the existing Agent Message Bus with SignalR fan-out. The bus stays inert — observability spine, not workflow engine; only the orchestrator acts, through existing APIs and bounded, policy-versioned actions with idempotent receipts. Pilot in Coding Agent Chat with the single category chat.message-send.failed ending in one idempotent Backlog healing card. Seven decisions are on the table (architecture, truth boundaries, Agent Studio as operations hub, bus authority, pilot, local-only privacy, rollout order); the dossier recommends approving all seven.

Evidence

  • Current-state inventory of 9 existing signal families with concrete code locations (GitProcessTelemetry with its 1,500 ms slow-spawn warning, ProductRuntimeEventStore JSONL streams, AgentMessageBusStore + SignalR fan-out, runner journals, review evidence, prompt-call telemetry, wiki-usage store, pipeline alarms) — the building blocks exist, a common org-level event contract and action loop do not.
  • Bus-option comparison: file-first is durable but slow-feedback; local-push-only risks loss or app blocking during Studio outages and is rejected as sole architecture; hybrid gives atomic file append plus immediate push when reachable.
  • Proposed policy thresholds are initial rules, not measured baselines (error cluster: same fingerprint 3 times across 2 correlations in 30 minutes or one Fatal; friction: 5 times across 3 correlations in 7 days; retention 30 days raw / 3 days Trace-Debug).
  • Known gaps the dossier flags itself: Quality Studio and voice-lint are absent from the dated Project Map capture; AGT-2652 found inspected nightly task records with null tokenSummary — token durability and coverage are uneven.

Cost of delay

No per-week number is quantified. Qualitatively: cross-app defect recurrence is never clustered into one operational signal (errors, friction, warnings stay scattered across request logs, task folders, runner state, review files, and feature-specific alarm projections), and the already-approved Global Orchestrator Watcher (AGT-2557) is missing its cross-product trigger substrate — each week of delay keeps repeated failures invisible as clusters and blocks that guardian vision. The dossier is honest that no measured incident or volume numbers back the urgency.

On approval

Slices T0–T4 in order: T0 publishes the OrgTelemetryEvent/v1 schema, event-name registry, and privacy test fixtures; T1 instruments only chat.message-send.failed in the Coding Agent Chat workbench (app-local JSONL, no content leakage, unchanged behavior without Studio); T2 hybrid delivery with collector checkpoints, replay, and file/push parity; T3 one action — a pure error-cluster policy creating exactly one idempotent Backlog healing card, validated on 10 synthetic and 5 real cases with zero leaks and duplicates; T4 a two-week observe-only soak with a 95%-useful-cards exit gate. Then rollout: Quality Studio, Agent Studio + Runner, Token Economy + websites, Agent Studio website, voice-lint last. Default action mode stays observe; implementation cards still need to be cut.

Open questions

  • Quality Studio and voice-lint have no stable appId or event root in the current Project Map capture — their onboarding shape is asserted, not evidenced from their repositories.
  • All clustering thresholds, cooldowns, and budgets are unvalidated initial rules; only the T4 soak produces measurements (false clusters, card acceptance, disk use).
  • The hybrid's two delivery paths require parity tests the dossier itself names as the option's cost — dedupe correctness across crash, rotation, torn-tail, and duplicate wake-up cases is assumed, not demonstrated.

§7AGT-W35 · Task Timeline redesign

confidence: high

Dossier: docs/operations/timeline-redesign/ (card AGT-2631)

Recommendation

Adopt one presentation contract for the Task Timeline: six event categories in one shared row grid with three structural row registers; incident state projected from unresolved open events — a banner exists only while an incident is open and is closed by a typed, linked resolution event; newest-first day sections with stable anchors; one centrally maintained technical-concept glossary behind explanation-first copyable popovers. AGT-2631 contains no implementation — approval converts seven specified delivery slices (T1–T7) into delivery cards; rejection should name which of decisions D1–D4 changes.

Evidence

No per-week or monetary figure — operator time lost is not quantified. On approval

A verification matrix is specified; no owners are named in the dossier.

Open questions

  • The "after" state is unproven: all four AGT-2577 after-mockups are explicitly "mocked proposal" SVGs, and T7's acceptance depends on a measured long-stream baseline and an agreed render/scroll budget the dossier does not yet define.
  • T4 requires a new backend ledger contract (durable eventId, incidentId, resolutionOf, closure kinds) plus new closure-producing events such as "Quota restored"; closure rules per incident type leave judgment calls to implementation.
  • No effort estimate per slice and no quantified operator impact — the decision rests on well-documented qualitative evidence.
